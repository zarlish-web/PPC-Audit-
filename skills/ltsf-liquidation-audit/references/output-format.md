## 1. The three deliverables

| Deliverable | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The audit document** | The written analysis. Every decision defended with the number it rests on |
| **The decided bulk** | The product's bulk with Action, Reasoning, New Bids, New Budget, New Percentage populated on every changed row, plus new-campaign build sheets |
| **Decisions requested** | Numbered, each blocking something named in the audit, each with an owner and a date |

The audit and the bulk must reference each other **by exact filename and version**. A plan citing a superseded workbook is untraceable the moment anyone asks why a bid was set, which is the moment it matters most.

## 2. The audit document spine

### 0 · Header and the plan in five lines
Product, scope, marketplace, declared objective, date. Then the whole plan in a handful of lines, each finding carrying the number it rests on. A reader who stops here knows what is wrong, what it costs, and what is being asked of them.

### 1 · Objective and operating rules
What the objective is, what it displaces, and the rules the run is bound by. State plainly that rank, share and defence metrics do not apply to these units.

### 2 · Where the product stands
Current position on today's data, with the readable window named and the unreadable ones excluded.

### 3 · Context corrections — read before any verdict below
The numbered Readings from Step 2, each naming the raw conclusion it reverses. **This section comes before every section that would otherwise state one of those conclusions.**

### 4 · The loss and opportunity audit
Every lever quantified per month, **ranked by size regardless of which team owns it**, each with an owner, a basis and whether it is fixable now. Ends with a stated total and the advertising share of it.

Then workstream ownership — which workstreams exist, their monthly value, and which have no owner. A workstream that does not exist is a finding, and it is not an advertising finding.

Ranking by size rather than by owner is what keeps the audit honest. An advertising plan that addresses a small share of the quantified loss should say so in its own words rather than letting its length imply otherwise.

### 5 · Cost structure and the adopted ceiling
The line-by-line build to one **ceiling adopted** figure, the acceleration window stated, current ad cost per unit, and headroom.

### 6 · Inventory, fees and the clock
Units by bracket, the cliff dates, the runway, and what is in flight. Days of cover per child and where budget currently sits against where stock sits.

### 7 · Price and offer architecture
Realised price against list, effective discount, deal cadence and what each window measured. Where deals are unmeasured, say how many ran and how many were read.

### 8 · Traffic and exposure
Syntax-level position against market. Size, colour and attribute against stock — where stock and demand do not line up. This is where stock that cannot be cleared by any keyword gets named.

### 9 · Listing, conversion and retention
Content state, and what comes back. Where returns concentrate on the same children holding the most stock, name the conflict rather than letting the clearance plan quietly assume it away.

### 10 · Competitive read
The set priced and dated; how they buy traffic; ad-type mix. **Source limits stated on the face of it** where the data is a partial scrape.

### 11 · Advertising diagnosis
Placement, lanes, match types and keywords. Spend, orders, cost per order and a decision per lane.

### 12 · What we did and what it produced
Prior actions graded against their predictions. **Written even when there is nothing to grade, with the reason** — a missing scoring table reads identically to a scoring step that was skipped.

Build status per syntax and match type: is each live, or built and not delivering? Coverage and delivery are different questions.

Advertised-SKU register and provenance.

### 13 · The clearability verdict
Months to clear at current velocity against the runway, then the gate result: open, split, closed or unmeasured. On a closed lane this section carries the five numbers and the audit stops building.

### 14 · Changes to the existing account
Row counts by change type, with the evidence for each.

### 15 · New campaigns
The build set, the separation rules as applied, and the staging-check results.

### 16 · Budget and what spend buys
The spend-to-outcome curve with **degraded** click price and conversion at each level — not straight-line extrapolation, because click price climbs as bidding widens and conversion falls as targeting loosens.

Then the chosen level with its reason. **Where the chosen level is not the one maximising net benefit, say so and give the reason.** Clearing faster has value the storage arithmetic alone does not capture, and that is a legitimate choice stated openly.

### 17 · Oversight and the grading calendar
Daily and weekly checks, what is compared against what, where divergence routes. Prediction scoring with its escalation rule. **Settle windows** — where two levers deploy together, say what the first read may and may not claim.

### 18 · Reconciliation, gaps and what would change the plan
- Figures to reconcile
- Named gaps, each with why it matters, what is needed, and an owner
- Caveats and what the data could not show
- **What would reverse the main verdicts**
- Decisions requested — numbered, each blocking something named above

## 3. Writing standards

These carry more of the quality than the section list does.

| # | Standard | Why |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Every table states what it decides** | A table without a stated consequence is data, not analysis. If nothing follows from it, it does not belong |
| W2 | **Every number carries a basis** — measured here, inferred from market, or from a test | An untagged multiplier is not a weak estimate; it is no estimate, and it cannot be scored later |
| W3 | **Measured and modelled effects are separated** | A modelled figure names its assumption in the row rather than absorbing it into a headline |
| W4 | **State what the analysis deliberately does not claim** | A projection resting on an unnamed coefficient reads more certain than it is |
| W5 | **Empty sections are written, with the reason** | An omitted section is indistinguishable from a skipped step |
| W6 | **A hold is a decision, not the absence of one** | The reasons a row may be held without asking are a closed list; anything else is asked about first. A hold must not become the path of least resistance because it requires nothing further to happen |
| W7 | **Overturned claims are kept with the evidence that overturned them** | Otherwise the same wrong claim returns next cycle and is re-argued from scratch |
| W8 | **A downgraded finding is stated as downgraded**, with the decomposition | Silently dropping it loses the reasoning and invites its return |
| W9 | **Corrections come before verdicts** | A correction placed after the section it reverses reverses nothing |
| W10 | **A verdict repeating across several cycles without producing its effect is a diagnosis error, not patience** | The repetition itself is the finding. A row graded flat for the fourth time is a different decision from one graded flat for the first, even where the numbers look identical |
| W11 | **Depth has a stated scale limit** | Full argued depth across hundreds of rows cannot be produced reliably. Produce it in batches, highest-value first, and say which rows got it |
| W12 | **No sentence without a number. No threshold that does not trace to its authority** | Banned outright: "optimise", "monitor", and any recommendation to raise a bid above the ceiling |

## 4. The decided bulk

Preserve every original sheet and column, in order. Add only what the decision requires.

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Action | The concrete change with prior and new values — "cut bid $0.50 to $0.30", not "reduce bid" |
| Reasoning | Why that action follows, with the numbers it rests on |
| New Bids / New Budget / New Percentage | Populated on every changed row, blank on unchanged rows |

New campaign builds go on appended sheets, one per ad product, carrying entity, operation, campaign, ad group, targeting, state, budget, match type, keyword, bid, SKU and a note.

Rows outside this product are untouched, and the audit says so.

## 5. The validation gate

Run before anything ships. Every failure is fixed, not noted.

**Traceability**
1. The audit names the decided bulk by exact filename and version, and that file exists.
2. Row counts quoted in the audit match the file.
3. Campaign counts quoted in the audit match the file.
4. Every budget figure quoted in the audit matches the file's total.

**Internal consistency**
5. No figure appears with two different values in two places. Where a superseded figure is retained deliberately, it is labelled as superseded.
6. The decisions-requested list asks for the numbers the plan is actually built on — not figures from an earlier version.
7. Every decision requested blocks something named in the body.

**Decision integrity**
8. Every changed row has both an Action and a Reasoning.
9. Every Reasoning contains at least one number.
10. No Reasoning contains "optimise" or "monitor".
11. No staged bid exceeds the ceiling.
12. The ceiling is dated later than all of its inputs.

**Structure**
13. The context-corrections section appears before every verdict it affects.
14. Every table states what it decides.
15. Every prediction carries a basis and a dated read.
16. Named gaps, what would reverse the main verdicts, and decisions requested are all present and non-empty — or explicitly stated as empty with the reason.

Checks 1 to 6 are the ones that fail most often in practice, and they fail quietly. A plan whose lead decision asks approval for a budget the plan abandoned two sections earlier will be approved for the wrong number, and nobody notices until the spend lands.
