# The leak audit

Step 2. The step that decides whether the rest of the document is worth writing.

On an aged product the loss is rarely mostly advertising. Price, refunds, storage and conversion are usually larger, and a plan that opens with campaigns has already conceded the argument. Quantify everything first, rank it, then say what share of the total the PPC plan can reach.

## The rule that makes it useful

**Every leak is priced in dollars per month, or it is declared unquantifiable.** A leak described qualitatively cannot be ranked against one carrying a number, so it silently loses — which is how the largest problem on a product ends up in a paragraph while the tenth-largest gets a table.

Where a leak cannot be priced, say so in its row, name what would price it, and give it an owner. That row is then a named gap, not a gesture.

## The ten lever groups

**1 · Cost structure.** Landed COGS against unit COGS, FBA fulfilment, cube and size tier, referral, refund cost per unit. Price the gap between what the model assumes and what Sellerboard realises. A landed cost held in the wrong place is worth more than most bid changes.

**2 · Inventory and the clock.** Days of cover per SKU, storage and aged surcharge per unit per month, age bands and the next bracket boundary, and whether approved removals were actually filed. Price the monthly charge, and price the charge that crosses the next bracket if nothing changes.

**3 · Price and offer.** Position on the price ladder against the verified competitor set, realised versus list, promotional depth, deal calendar, which offer types are even enabled. Price the contribution given away per unit at current depth. A discount cuts contribution close to its full depth, because COGS and the non-referral fees do not fall with price.

**4 · Traffic.** Impression share, keyword coverage against the master list, syntax and size index, which surfaces are used against which are available. Price the volume implied by the share gap at market conversion.

**5 · Click.** Brand CTR against market CTR, main image, title, badges. Price the clicks forgone at the market rate on the impressions already held — this is usually the cheapest large leak on the list, because the impressions are already paid for.

**6 · Conversion.** Brand CVR against market, purchases per impression, click-to-cart, content completeness, review count and rating, variation structure. Price the orders forgone at market conversion on current clicks.

**7 · Retention.** Refund rate by SKU, sellable quota, reason codes, deal lag. Price the refunded contribution plus the handling, and note that refunded units re-enter the aged pool and re-accrue charge — so on a clearance product a refund costs twice.

**8 · PPC efficiency.** Placement pricing, syntax mix, SKU routing, zero-order spend past the click line, harvest backlog, match mix, budget utilisation. Price the spend above ceiling, not the spend.

**9 · Demand beyond search.** Deal cadence and whether uplift was ever measured, off-Amazon traffic, multi-channel fulfilment. Price the units a deal moves against the contribution it gives away.

**10 · Execution.** Whether decided changes are in the account and approved removals were filed. Price what the undone work would have delivered. This row is frequently non-zero and it is always uncomfortable.

## The ranked table

| Leak | $/month | Share of total | Owner | Basis | Fixable now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank by size. Not by which team is in the room, not by what is easiest, not by what this document is about.

Then, immediately beneath it, one line that the reader cannot miss:

> **The PPC plan in this document addresses X of the $Y monthly loss — Z%.**

If Z is small, that sentence belongs in the opening summary too. A reader who acts only on the PPC section while the top three leaks stand is doing the wrong work, and the document is responsible for telling them.

## Owners

Every row carries a named owner. PPC owns lever 8 and part of 4. Everything else routes out:

| Lever | Typical owner |
|---|---|
| Cost structure, price, offer | Brand Management |
| Inventory, removals, disposition | LTSF owner |
| Click and conversion assets | Creative / Listing |
| Retention and reason codes | Quality |
| Deals | Brand Management |
| Execution | Whoever the action was assigned to |

A leak with no owner is itself a finding, and it is reported as one.

## A leak is only real if it is still leaking

**RULED 4 September 2026, after making the same error twice on one product.**

Two of the largest PPC leaks in a live plan were already closed before the plan was written, and both were counted anyway:

| Leak | Claimed | Actually released | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad spend on SKUs that clear unaided | $2,414/month | **$0.00** | Closed by cutting budget **caps** the account reaches 4.4% of |
| Keywords with clicks and zero orders | $2,156/month | **$0.00** | The 43 keywords were **already paused**, before the plan existed |

**$4,570/month of claimed recovery, none of it real.** The first was the wrong lever. The second was the right lever already pulled.

**Before a leak enters the table, prove it is still flowing:**

1. **Is the spend actually happening now?** Not enabled, not capped — spent, in the last window, on rows that are live.
2. **Is the fix a lever that moves money?** A cap the lane never reaches releases nothing. A pause on something already paused releases nothing.
3. **State the release against actual spend**, never against the cap or against a historical figure.

A leak that fails any of the three is written into the plan as **already closed**, with the date and who closed it — never deleted, because next cycle needs to know it was checked, and never counted, because counting it inflates the recovery and hides the leaks that are real.
