# Product Audit and Plan — Format Standard

**Draft v0.1 · for review**

Derived by comparing two live documents built to different objectives:

- **Cooling Comforter PPC Plan v1.1** (27 Aug 2026) — ranking objective. 9 sections, 68 tables.
- **SLQS Product Audit and Clearance Plan** (28 Aug 2026) — clearance objective. 18 sections, 33 tables.

Neither is complete on its own. The ranking plan has the stronger **reasoning spine**; the clearance audit has the stronger **economics and loss accounting**. This standard is the union, with the objective-specific parts named as such.

---

## Part 1 — What each document does better

### The ranking plan's spine, missing from the clearance audit

| Section | What it does | Why it matters on any objective |
|---|---|---|
| **Context-corrected metrics** | States corrections that each *reverse* a conclusion the raw numbers would support — wrong scope, wrong entity, contaminated comparison windows, a metric read on the wrong term | The single highest-value section in either document. Without it an audit confidently reports the wrong thing |
| **Prior actions — what there is to grade** | Scores last cycle's predictions before making new decisions | Stops a lever that stopped working from being repeated on the assumption it still works |
| **Build status vs coverage** | Coverage asks whether a match type exists; build status asks whether it is *live or built and not delivering* | A built-but-silent campaign reads as coverage and buys nothing |
| **Advertised-SKU register and provenance** | Which child was actually enabled over the window the performance history spans | Performance history attributed to the wrong child is worse than no history |
| **Duplicate ownership** | Which campaign owns a term, stated | Self-competition and unreadable attribution |
| **Oversight** | Daily checks, weekly order, divergence routing, prediction scoring, settle windows | A plan reviewed weekly is a plan running itself |
| **Contingencies and standing playbooks** | Dated re-entry plans; what a recovery differs from a fresh start | A hold with no stated resumption reads as abandonment by the time conditions change |
| **Figures to reconcile** | Numbers that disagree across sources, named | An unreconciled figure is a future argument |
| **What would reverse the main verdicts** | Falsification conditions for each headline claim | Makes the plan checkable rather than merely assertive |

### The clearance audit's economics, missing from the ranking plan

| Section | What it does | Why it matters on any objective |
|---|---|---|
| **The leak audit** | Every lever quantified in dollars per month, **ranked by size, not by which team owns it**, with an owner and a basis tag per leak, and a stated total | Puts advertising in proportion. This audit's own total showed advertising as 15% of quantified loss and said so |
| **Workstream ownership** | Which workstreams exist, their monthly value, and which read **NO WORKSTREAM OPEN** | Names organisational gaps that no advertising action can close |
| **Cost structure to an adopted ceiling** | Price, fees, refund cost, contribution, then the objective-specific addition, ending in one **CEILING ADOPTED** figure with headroom against current spend | One number every later decision is judged against |
| **The spend-to-outcome curve** | Several spend levels with **degraded** cost per click and conversion at each, and the net benefit of each | Prevents straight-line extrapolation, and makes the chosen level a stated choice rather than a default |

## Part 2 — The unified structure

Universal sections carry **U**. Objective-specific sections carry **O** and swap contents by objective.

### 0 · Header and the plan in five lines
Product, scope, marketplace, **declared objective**, date. Then the whole plan in a handful of lines, each finding carrying the number it rests on. A reader who stops here should know what is wrong, what it costs and what is being asked of them.

### 1 · The product story and context-corrected metrics — **U**
- **1.1 History** — what shaped performance, including causes outside advertising
- **1.2 Current situation on today's data**
- **1.3 Context corrections — read before any verdict below.** Each correction stated as a numbered *Reading*, naming the raw conclusion it reverses and the evidence that reverses it. Where a correction can be settled against an independent source, state **the check that settles it**
- **1.4 Inventory, days of cover and SKU routing** — with dated re-entry plans for every held child

### 2 · What we did and what it produced — **U**
- **2.1 Performance at parent level**, with the readable window named and the unreadable ones excluded
- **2.2 Objective outcome** — rank for a ranking objective, units cleared and charge avoided for a clearance objective
- **2.3 Prior actions — what there is to grade.** Written **even when there is nothing to grade**, with the reason. A missing scoring table reads identically to a scoring step that was skipped
- **2.4 Build status per syntax and match type** — live, or built and not delivering
- **2.5 Spend resolving to stock that cannot be sold**
- **2.6 Advertised-SKU register and provenance**

### 3 · The loss and opportunity audit — **U**
Every lever quantified per month, **ranked by size regardless of owner**, each with an owner, a basis tag and whether it is fixable now. Ends with a stated total and the advertising share of it.
- **3.1 Value not being captured**
- **3.2 Workstream ownership**, including workstreams that do not exist

### 4 · Cost structure and the adopted ceiling — **U**, contents **O**
Per unit: realised price, fees, refund cost, contribution before advertising, then the objective's own addition, ending in **CEILING ADOPTED** and headroom against current ad cost per unit.

| Objective | The addition to contribution |
|---|---|
| Clearance / LTSF | Storage avoided over a **stated bounded acceleration window**, never the whole projected hold |
| Ranking | The sized push allowance, with its loss ceiling and horizon |
| Profitable conversion | Nothing. The ceiling is contribution |

### 5 · Constraint clock — **O**
What is running out and when. Cliff dates and runway for clearance; inventory cover and stockout dates for ranking.

### 6 · Diagnosis — **U**
- **6.1 Syntax** — where impression share and conversion sit against the market, per syntax
- **6.2 Placement** — spend share, cost per order and which placements clear their ceiling
- **6.3 Lanes and match types** — spend, orders, cost per order, decision per lane
- **6.4 Size, colour and attribute against stock** — where stock and demand do not line up
- **6.5 Cross-comparison against siblings** in the same catalogue

### 7 · Competitive read — **U**
The set priced and dated; how they buy traffic; ad-type mix and off-search share. **Source limits stated on the face of it** where the scrape is partial.

### 8 · Per-keyword and per-campaign mathematics — **U**
Objective declared per campaign. Then per keyword above the oversight line: target, required clicks per day, expected conversion and click price, derived budget. Plus **how each budget is derived** and **duplicate ownership**.

### 9 · Coverage — **U**
The whole keyword universe against what is targeted; the highest-value gaps; paused terms with reason or reactivation; named declines; **ad-type coverage** and the plan to close it.

### 10 · The plan — **U**, contents **O**
- **10.1 Changes to the existing account**, with row counts
- **10.2 New campaigns**, with the isolation rule stated
- **10.3 Budget and what spend buys** — the spend-to-outcome curve with **degraded** inputs at each level, then the chosen level with its reason. Where the chosen level is not the one maximising net benefit, **say so and give the reason**

### 11 · Oversight — **U**
Daily checks on the campaigns carrying most spend. The weekly order, and why the order is not arbitrary. **Divergence routing.** Prediction scoring, with the escalation rule. **Settle windows and read dates**, including where two levers deployed together make a read unattributable.

### 12 · Contingencies and standing playbooks — **U**
Dated re-entry for every held row. Recovery versus fresh start. Assets currently unexamined.

### 13 · Execution register — **U**
Waves with dates. **One named owner per line** — shared ownership is no ownership. The value recovered and the measure that says it worked. Each row states the trade-off in the terms a reviewer decides on. Recommendations to other teams kept separate, raised with evidence, never worked around with a bid.

### 14 · Reconciliation, gaps and what would change the plan — **U**
- **14.1 What the workbook changed** — plan-level decisions that did not survive row-level work, and why
- **14.2 What is held, by layer, and why each** — reasons stated separately so the set does not read as one blanket freeze
- **14.3 Figures to reconcile**
- **14.4 Named gaps** — each with why it matters, what is needed, and an owner
- **14.5 Caveats and what the data could not show**
- **14.6 What would reverse the main verdicts**
- **14.7 Decisions requested** — numbered, each blocking something named above, each with an owner and a date

## Part 3 — Writing standards

These are what separate the two documents in quality, more than the section list does.

| # | Standard |
|---|---|
| W1 | **Every table states what it decides.** A table without a stated consequence is data, not analysis |
| W2 | **Every number carries a basis tag** — measured on this product, inferred from market, or from a test |
| W3 | **Measured and modelled effects are separated.** A modelled figure names its assumption in the row rather than absorbing it into a headline |
| W4 | **State what the analysis deliberately does not claim,** and why. A projection resting on an unnamed coefficient reads more certain than it is |
| W5 | **An empty section is written, not omitted,** with the reason it is empty |
| W6 | **A hold is a decision, not the absence of one.** The reasons a row may be held without asking are a closed list; anything else is asked about first |
| W7 | **Overturned claims are kept with the evidence that overturned them,** so the reasoning is not lost and is not re-argued next cycle |
| W8 | **A downgraded finding is stated as downgraded,** with the decomposition that downgraded it |
| W9 | **Corrections come before verdicts.** Any correction that reverses a raw-number conclusion appears before the section that would otherwise state it |
| W10 | **A repeated verdict that has not produced its effect over several consecutive cycles is a diagnosis error, not patience,** and escalates on the repetition itself |
| W11 | **Depth has a stated scale limit.** A fully argued case for every row cannot be produced reliably at roster scale; it is produced in small batches, highest-value first. The failure mode is not an obviously thin answer but a confidently thorough-looking one |
| W12 | **No sentence without a number.** No threshold that does not trace to its authority |

## Part 4 — What this changes for the LTSF work

Applied to the clearance audit reviewed alongside this, the following would be added:

1. **Context corrections.** The clearance audit has no equivalent section, and it needed one — its own companion-workbook reference and its lead budget decision were both carrying figures from a superseded version.
2. **Prior-cycle grading.** It has a forward grading calendar but scores nothing backward.
3. **Provenance.** It does not verify which child was enabled over the window its history spans.
4. **Duplicate ownership**, stated as a section rather than only as an isolation rule in the build.
5. **Figures to reconcile** and **what would reverse the main verdicts.**
6. **Settle windows.** Its deal window and price change overlap the read period, and the collision is mentioned but not carried into what the first read may claim.

And in the other direction, the ranking plan would gain the **leak audit with owners and a total**, the **workstream ownership table**, and the **adopted-ceiling table**, none of which it currently has.
