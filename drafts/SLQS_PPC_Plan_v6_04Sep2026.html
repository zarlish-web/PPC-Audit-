The plan in five lines

The product is not failing on conversion — it converts 50% better than the market per impression. It is failing on exposure, holding 0.194% impression share. The clearance answer is width: more terms, more surfaces, bids kept low in the subsidy zone, and budgets left alone.

The previous cycle's largest reported saving was not real. $2,176/day of budget cap was withdrawn and released $4.88/day, because the account spends 4.4% of the cap it already has. The money that is recoverable — $3,698/month — sits at keyword level on SKUs that clear without help, and this version moves it there.

548 rows of the decided bulk are corrected. The headline economics stand; the levers were wrong.

The subsidy zone is $0.475 to $0.982. 162 of 455 bids sit inside it deliberately — the sale loses money and the avoided storage pays for it. At the highest such bid the subsidy is $18.12/unit against $14.70 avoided, which is why the per-keyword test governs, not the blended one:

max affordable bid = current bid × (ceiling ÷ that keyword's own CPA)

One bid exceeds the blended $0.982 cap: ivory quilt queen at $1.10. It converts at $7.86 CPA, where the affordable bid is $2.64. It is comfortably inside. Testing it on blended CVR would have wrongly cut the best keyword in the account.

A budget is a cap, not a spend. Cutting a cap the account reaches 4.4% of releases nothing, and 146 campaigns would have been left funded below the level at which a campaign can deliver a readable day — spending money and unable to learn from it.

All 270 cuts are reversed. Reversing them removed every floor breach on its own; no campaign now sits under $5.

Growth comes from targets, cost control from the bid. Neither is the budget. A lane spending 4.4% of its cap is short of reach — it gets more keywords, auto groups, category and product targeting, not a smaller cap.

237 campaigns carry SKUs with under 120 days of cover. Inside them: 251 keyword rows, 141 of them spending $77.85/day, plus product targeting. Total $123.25/day.

Every one is reachable at target level — no low-cover campaign lacks keyword rows — so this is closed on the rows themselves, at the $0.25 floor, and the caps are left alone.

Seven keywords convert at 12.7% to 32.6% ACoS — below the 33.7% break-even, so profitable. The ladder does not touch a profitable lane. They are cut anyway, and correctly:

The prior plan made White the growth story of the colour build. It is a FLOOR-tier SKU and cannot be. A refunded unit re-enters the aged pool and re-accrues the charge, so a refund costs twice. White keeps its coverage and is not weighted up.

The forward-cash argument for pushing anyway is sound — storage costs $146,313 over the runway against roughly $22,000 of annualised refund cost — but it answers a different question. The gate does not say stop advertising; it says do not scale the SKU that comes back.

Two supporting cautions: the refund rate is a rising series (0.5% → 17.6% → 15.8% → 18.2% → 26.0%), which a point-in-time tier misreads; and no unit counts were supplied, so the sample guard is unchecked.
