The product is not failing on conversion — it converts 50% better than the market per impression. It is failing on **exposure**, holding 0.194% impression share. The clearance answer is width: more terms, more surfaces, bids kept low in the subsidy zone, and budgets left alone.

The previous cycle's largest reported saving was not real. **$2,176/day of budget cap was withdrawn and released $4.88/day**, because the account spends 4.4% of the cap it already has. The money that *is* recoverable — **$3,698/month** — sits at keyword level on SKUs that clear without help, and this version moves it there.

**548 rows of the decided bulk are corrected.** The headline economics stand; the levers were wrong.

## 3 · The economics — four numbers, two anchors

Everything downstream is derived from these. The prior plan computed only the ceiling.

| | Value | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution per unit, after refunds | $13.77 | HIST |
| Storage avoided, 2 months at $7.35 | +$14.70 | HIST |
| **Ceiling per unit** | **$28.47** | The forward-cash limit |
| **Break-even ACoS** | **33.7%** | Below this the sale is profitable. **Never cut here on performance** |
| **Ceiling ACoS** | **69.6%** | Above this, always correct — orders are no defence |
| Floor bid | $0.25 | Below this a bid wins nothing |
| **Max profitable CPC** | **$0.475** | Where profit ends, at 3.45% blended CVR |
| **Max click price** | **$0.982** | Where forward cash ends |

**The subsidy zone is $0.475 to $0.982.** 162 of 455 bids sit inside it deliberately — the sale loses money and the avoided storage pays for it. At the highest such bid the subsidy is $18.12/unit against $14.70 avoided, which is why **the per-keyword test governs, not the blended one**:

**max affordable bid = current bid × (ceiling ÷ that keyword's own CPA)**

One bid exceeds the blended $0.982 cap: *ivory quilt queen* at $1.10. It converts at **$7.86 CPA**, where the affordable bid is **$2.64**. It is comfortably inside. Testing it on blended CVR would have wrongly cut the best keyword in the account.

**One genuine breach was found and corrected** — *white quilt king size*, raised to $0.65 at a $25.90 CPA where $0.55 is the limit.

### 7.1 The click line

| Bucket | Rows | Prior action | This plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15–98 clicks, zero orders | 43 | Pause | **Pause — held.** Median 21 clicks. None priced under $0.15, so the 20–25 extension does not apply |

| 1–14 clicks, under-120-day SKU | **94** | Cut 40% | **Cut to the $0.25 floor**, re-argued as a stock decision |

**The distinction that resolves it.** The click line bars *performance* judgements on thin data. A low-cover cut is not a performance judgement — it is a stock one, in the same class as negating an irrelevant term on sight at any click count. Where a keyword was cut for both reasons, the stock reason governs and the reasoning now says so.

The prior file's own words made the case: *"3 clicks over 30 days. Too thin to judge. Small trim."* Too thin to judge, and judged anyway.

### 7.2 The correction ladder

A flat 40% was applied to all 16 above-ceiling keywords. Their ACoS ranges **70.7% to 150.8%** — all above the 69.6% ceiling ACoS, so all correctly cut, but not all by the same amount.

### 7.3 The two anchors in practice

Seven keywords convert at **12.7% to 32.6% ACoS** — below the 33.7% break-even, so profitable. The ladder does not touch a profitable lane. They are cut anyway, and correctly:

**The SKUs clear without paid support, so we would be paying for units that were going to sell regardless.** That is an incrementality argument, not an efficiency one, and it is the one case that overrides break-even protection. The reasoning on all seven now says which lever it is — a cut that reads as efficiency when it is really incrementality gets reversed next cycle for the wrong reason.
