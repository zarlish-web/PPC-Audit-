The plan in five lines

Nothing from the last cycle was deployed. The v5 decided file was produced on 1 September and held for feedback, so the account today sits exactly where it did before that plan was written. There is no prior cycle to grade on outcomes, and two leaks the last plan claimed as recoverable are not.

The product is not failing on conversion — it converts 50% better than the market per impression. It fails on exposure, at 0.194% impression share. The clearance answer is width: more terms, more surfaces, bids low in the subsidy zone, budgets left alone.

The largest PPC item still open is the one the last plan marked done. The Top-of-Search premium is live at a 122.5% mean across 327 campaigns, worth $1,977/month.

585 rows are decided and the file passes all twenty validation checks. What changed since v6 is not the economics — it is the catalogue, the relevance tiering, the competitor scan and the duplicate check, which between them removed 61 keywords that should never have been proposed.

One number is deliberately not stated: the daily budget. It depends on a coverage calculation whose inputs are not yet derived. Section 4.2.

Reading 4 — a leak is only real if it is still leaking

The v6 plan claimed $8,213/month of PPC recovery. The real figure is $4,589. Three separate errors, in both directions:

  • Leak 6, $2,156 — already closed. Those 43 keywords were paused before the plan was written. Pausing them again releases nothing.

  • Leak 5, $2,324 not $3,698. My own v6 figure counted keyword rows sitting inside paused campaigns. A paused campaign spends nothing going forward, whatever its history shows. The original $2,414 estimate was closer than my correction to it.

Advertising is $4,589/month, or 11.3% of what is still flowing. The other $35,902 sits with brand management, supply and product development. Storage alone is 4.4× everything advertising can fix.

Three of five workstreams still have no owner. Returns ($3,540) and pricing ($2,983) are each larger than every PPC lever combined, and neither has an open workstream. That remains the largest finding in this audit and it is not an advertising finding.

The subsidy zone is $0.475 to $0.982, and the per-keyword test governs, not the blended one:

max affordable bid = current bid × (ceiling ÷ that keyword's own CPA)

One bid exceeds the blended cap — ivory quilt queen at $1.10, converting at $7.86 CPA where the affordable bid is $2.64. Comfortably inside. 4.2 The recommended figure is not stated in this version, and why

Ruled 4 September: on aged stock, deals and PPC share the work — sometimes both, sometimes PPC alone. Which one applies is computable per segment, not a matter of opinion:

coverage ratio = suppliable pace ÷ required pace

RatioVerdictHow PPC is sized
1.0 and abovePPC can leadTo the full required pace
0.4 to 1.0PPC sharesTo the suppliable pace — the gap is a named deal or pricing requirement
Under 0.4Deals must leadFloor bids for coverage only

Run on three segments, this product returns three answers:

SegmentPoolRequiredSuppliableRatioVerdict
Whole product2,516 units41.9/day28.7/day0.68PPC shares

Light grey holds 36% of the pool against 84 monthly searches. At full impression share that is under two units a month. No bid moves it. It needs deal depth or an off-Amazon route, and this plan funds it at floor for coverage only.

The whole-product ratio of 0.68 means PPC is sized to the suppliable pace, not the required one — and on the curve above that is nearer $400/day than the $762 v6 asked for.

That number is not stated here because its inputs are not derived. The addressable-search and reachable-share figures behind 28.7/day are estimates built to demonstrate the test, not read from the SQP file. Decision 1: derive them, then set the budget. Until then the build is specified and unfunded.

4.3 The real ceiling is stock, not spend
