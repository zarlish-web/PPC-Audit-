## The plan in six lines

**Black is being advertised at roughly twice what it can carry.** Its remaining 80 charge-bearing units clear in about six weeks, so its avoidable charge — and therefore its allowable ad cost — is about **$8/unit**. It is being advertised at **$16.84**, and it took 64% of the spend.

**White is the larger remaining job** — 153 charge-bearing units against Black's 80, four and a half months of cover, and **69% of the charge**. Its ceiling is about $19/unit and it is inside it.

**The budget is not the constraint. Reach is.** $343.73/day sits enabled and only **11%** of it spends. The lane is nine times short on impressions, not short of money.

**52% of the enabled budget is in campaigns that cannot serve.** The 18-campaign expansion book spent roughly $20 in nineteen days; eleven of its campaigns drew under 200 impressions and two drew none.

**The cheap wide lanes are the only ones working, and they are starved.** The catch-all auto runs at 72% of a $15/day budget and produced 37% of all clicks. Broad runs at 96% of $5/day and has the lowest cost per order in the account.

**Exact takes 58% of keyword spend at the worst cost per order in the account** — $20.28 against Broad's $13.47.

## 1. Context corrections — read before any verdict below

Four corrections. Each reverses a conclusion the raw numbers support.

**Reading 1 — the charge file's product economics predate the plan that used them.**
The file carries price **$41.95** and landed cost **$18.86**. Price moved to $39.95 around 13/14 August and SellerBoard confirms $39.95 realised. Actual landed cost is **$21.36**. So the file's margin of $7.27/unit is overstated on both sides.
*The check that settles it:* SellerBoard cost of goods $1,260.24 ÷ 59 units = $21.36 exactly.

**Reading 2 — the 14 Aug plan's break-even line does not exist, and the error was inherited, not invented.**
The plan priced every bid against a **$5.57** break-even built from net proceeds $24.43 and landed cost $18.86 — the charge file's numbers. On actuals, contribution before advertising is **negative** at $39.95. The plan did not miscalculate; it read a stale source. Correcting the plan alone fixes nothing.

**Reading 3 — "651 aged units" is the wrong column, and the right one is 233.**
The charge file's aged-unit column counts units on hand. The column that governs the charge is **Total AIS Units: 153 White + 80 Black = 233**, carrying $849.89/month. Using 651 understates charge per unit by nearly two thirds and inflates months-to-clear beyond recognition.

**Reading 4 — the aged surcharge is already inside the Amazon fees line.**
Modelled fees $15.82/unit; actual $19.74. The $3.92 gap is the AIS charge at ~$3.65/unit. **So the charge is counted once, in fees — it cannot also be added back as "storage avoided" without double-counting.** Every ceiling below is built on contribution *excluding* storage, then adds avoidable future charge explicitly.

## 2. Cost structure and the two ceilings

Per unit, at the realised $39.95:

```
price                              $39.95
  − landed cost                    −$21.36    SellerBoard actual, not the file's $18.86
  − FBA fulfilment                  −$9.53
  − referral                        −$5.99
  = contribution before storage     +$3.07
```

The product does make money per unit before storage. What it cannot carry is storage plus advertising.

Allowable ad cost = contribution + the charge a unit avoids by selling now rather than at its current pace:

Months to clear is computed from **AIS units at realised velocity**, not from the charge file's DOH/MOH columns — those come from the same stale snapshot as its price and landed cost.

| | AIS units | Realised velocity | Months to clear | Charge/unit/mo | Avoidable | **Ceiling** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WHITE** | 153 | 1.15/day | ~4.4 | $3.84 | $16.90 | **$19.97** |
| **BLACK** | 80 | 1.80/day | ~1.5 | $3.28 | $4.92 | **$7.99** |

**This split is the most important number in the audit.** Both SKUs still carry charge, but Black clears its remaining pool in about six weeks against White's four and a half months — so Black has roughly a third of White's avoidable charge and therefore roughly a third of the allowable ad cost.

Against a blended actual of **$16.84 per ad-attributed unit**: White sits inside its ceiling; **Black runs at roughly twice its own.**

**Concentration:** three terms carry 18 of 39 orders and 42% of spend — *hanging closet organizer* ($17.01), *hanging shelves for closet* ($13.23), *hanging closet shelves* ($24.21).

**The cheap tail is the best value in the account.** Eight orders came in at **under $1 each — about $3 in total**, from the auto and catch-all lanes, including two conversions on competitor ASIN placements at $0.35 and $0.70. Set against ten orders costing $170 on the head term.

**On the 588 zero-order terms carrying $364.88 — do not mass-negate.**
At a 2.68% conversion rate a term needs roughly **37 clicks** before one order is even expected. The largest zero-order term has **26 clicks**. **Not one has reached the point where zero orders is evidence of failure.** Half the spend showing no orders is the arithmetic of a low-conversion lane, not waste. Negating now would remove the discovery surface that is producing the cheapest orders in the account.

## 12. What would reverse the main verdicts
