Feedback on the Day-1 review

A claim-by-claim check of boards 13a–13c, the automated-updates thread and the board's hourly data, plus the placement modifiers now live on broad and auto campaigns. Each point says whether the claim holds, why, and what the framework should change.

Checked Oct 7 · revised 12:30 PT after peer review (profit split re-based on the board's 09:46 PT read; Day-2 statements re-checked against the moves desk to M83, 10:04 PT; colour method aligned with the owner's rule; M47 treated as a planned test) · US only · times in PT unless marked CT
01

Takeaways, in priority order

  1. The headline profit beat is real, but the split between markets is wrong. (Corrected Oct 7 after the Sellerboard check.) On the board, about 78% of the "+87% vs plan" beat is Canada. On Sellerboard's costs, put on the same basis (refunds of earlier orders, a one-off FBA removal fee and liquidation income taken out, deal fees and the 7.5% reserve applied), the account totals are close ($8.3K vs the board's $9.8K after reserve, board read 09:46 PT Oct 7; the board itself moved $9,422 → $9,626 → $9,801 between reads), but the board under-states US profit by about $1.7K and over-states Canada by about $3.2K. So most of the beat is the US (about $7.5K after reserve vs $5.7K on the board). The line-by-line workings are in the decision analysis and its Excel file, and Canada's cost model needs reconciling before it is used for BFCM.
  2. The high modifiers on broad and auto are real, and the worst of them come back on Oct 8. The night-before T9 run, logged as "one move, no cap", set top of search (TOS) up to +900% and rest of search (ROS) up to +796%. The intraday desk brought most of them down, but six broad and auto campaigns that spent on Oct 6 still pay 2.8–6.6× their normal CPC for zero to two orders each. 22 campaigns are still above the 300% ceiling. The Oct 8 "reverts" don't restore pre-event values. They re-apply the T9 raises: the pending legs go from 0 back to the T9 value, e.g. +900% on the Twin LTSF auto, +731% on the B4 LTSF auto, and +441% / +380% on the two biggest B4 autos. Those two autos spent $640 on Oct 6. Cancel or rewrite those legs before Oct 8.
  3. The spike started the evening before the event, so it isn't in any Day-1 number. The event values went live Oct 5 19:00–22:00 PT. Across Oct 5–6, 14 autos paid $1,363 more than their normal CPC would have cost, and US spend on Oct 5 was $877 over a normal day. The board's $740 boost premium and its Day-1 P&L count Oct 6 only.
  4. The automated updates got the live numbers right and the projections wrong. Every live TACoS read (7.7–9.3%) was within a point of the close (8.8%). Every end-of-day projection (10.6–11.4%) was 1.8–2.6 points too high. The projections for Canada Satin 4pc and 6pc flagged break-even breaches that didn't happen. Profit estimates moved $9,071 → $6,678 → $10,054 as the method changed. A projection that assumes TACoS climbs late in the day should not be posted on an event day.
  5. TACoS did not climb late in the day. This answers Erik's question. Cumulative TACoS stayed between 8.3% and 9.3% from 07:00 PT to midnight. Evening ads were as efficient as the morning's (32% vs 33% ACoS). The late-day rise seen in Seller Central is its spend catching up, not ads getting worse. Read TACoS from complete hours of Marketing Stream data only.
  6. Several lessons rest on comparisons with no control. These include:
    • top of search vs rest of search, where the mix of campaigns differs and the ROS boosts inflate ROS CPC;
    • the placement cut, where nothing was left untouched to compare against;
    • the +200% boost CPC ($5.35 vs $2.41), which compares King and head terms with cheaper ones;
    • "hero-only deals lose", where the deal SKUs were picked because they were the best sellers;
    • satin's rank loss, which used a stale read.
    Treat these as hypotheses, and run holdouts on Day 2 or BFCM before they become rules.
  7. One Day-2 move breaks a Day-1 lesson; the others are tests or owner decisions. (Revised after peer review; checked against the moves desk to M83, 10:04 PT.)
    • Rule break: M48 re-enabled "bamboo cooling sheets" exact at +618%, with a revert queued to +718%, against L1 (boosts multiply CPC) and the 300% ceiling.
    • Planned test, not a break: M47 raised 16 push exacts by one step under a written ladder (×1.5 or +$10, never above a ceiling of plan clicks × 2 × CPC) and lowered top of search on 3 of them as test arm A. It is read on Oct 13 on 7-day median rank vs the week before, counted only beyond each keyword's noise band — the kind of controlled test this page asks for.
    • Owner decisions, not misses: top-of-search cuts (S2) were ruled out by the owner unless asked (M73, 07:58 PT), and the over-ceiling exacts are going to tonight's Oct 8 rollback review (M79). The LTSF satin broad's raise to $225 (M65) was inside its accepted cost at the time ($15.28 a unit); when it went over ($18.21 vs $17.07) M81 cut its bids 15% at 10:04 PT.
    The framework still needs the check: a move that breaks an active rule carries a named approver and a stated reason.
  8. The plan agreed in the planning thread wasn't the plan that ran. Joseph's go/no-go said ads would be consolidated on each product's main syntaxes and everything else paused Oct 6–7, checked hourly against the caps. Instead, 552 campaigns spent, 16 of them outside the event registry. US clearance (LTSF) campaigns spent $1,086. The caps weren't unrealistic: the model said uncapped ads would cost about $2.9K, and they did. Units beat plan by enough to cover it.
  9. The morning didn't pay best. Clicks between 03:00 and 10:00 PT converted to $9.04 of ad sales each but cost $2.96 while the boosts were live, so ACoS was 33%. The best hours were 11:00–14:00 PT, after the boosted campaigns had run out and the desk began cutting modifiers: 21% ACoS at $1.83 CPC. The evening ran at 32%. Lesson L8 is right that budgets should last all day, but not because mornings are more efficient.
  10. The numbers need one source of record. There are three ad-spend totals ($7,682 on the board, $7,821 in Sellerboard, $7,859 in ecotero) and two plan-profit definitions. Break-evens differ between boards (B4 17.8 / 17.6, B6 14.5 / 15.4). Day-1 profit moved $9,422 → $9,599 → $9,626 between reads. The closed day still shows an 18.8% end-of-day TACoS projection in the data file. Each of these changes a verdict somewhere.
  11. The planning thread shows the same pattern as the boards: decisions left to default. (Added after the planning audit.) Of the 15 critical-path steps, 1 was on time, 7 were late and 5 show no evidence of being done, including the "+50% event bid rule" check. Nobody approved or refused Umair's 13 deal cancellations, so five deals ran unplanned. Nobody decided the Satin PED, so it switched on by default at 19:40 PT Oct 6 against the board's recommendation. Six of Erik's questions were never answered. The framework needs a named owner and a deadline for every open decision, with an explicit default if nobody decides.

Erik's questions in the planning thread, answered from the data

  • "Why does TACoS rise toward the end of the day: delay, or worse efficiency?" Delay. On Day 1, cumulative TACoS from complete hours was 9.35% at 07:00 PT, 8.75% at 14:00 and 8.95% at 23:00. It was flat. Evening ads ran 32% ACoS vs 33% in the morning. Seller Central shows spend later than orders, so TACoS read there climbs as spend catches up. Fix: read TACoS from complete Marketing Stream hours, and never project a late climb on an event day.
  • "Spend should go on the variation with the highest click rate." The evidence is mixed, so this is still an open question. On Day 1 the hero had the higher CTR on the same search term in 3 of 4 comparisons, but across different campaigns and placements. The board's 30-day shared-ad-group data (M56) shows the opposite: other colours draw more clicks (CTR 0.3–1.2% vs 0.2–0.6% for the hero) and convert worse. Neither is a clean test. The owner's rule since 04:35 PT Oct 7 judges cost per order first, conversion second, only above a volume floor (100+ clicks a side). The measure that combines Erik's front-facing point with conversion is orders per 1,000 impressions (CTR × conversion, counting orders of any colour), then cost per order. The clean test: hero vs one other colour on the same exact keyword, in single-ad ad groups, same bid and placement. Against a normal day (Sep 22–Oct 4, same per-SKU method) hero share was B4 71%, B6 58%, S4 63%; on Day 1 it was 52%, 60%, 51%. Without LTSF, B6 and S4 were at or above normal, so only B4 fell for non-LTSF reasons (the Full Grey discovery auto and stock swaps).
  • "Run a PED on only the front-facing ranking variations?" Day 1 doesn't answer this. L7 ("hero-only deals lose") compares the weaker siblings with whole parents. The test that answers it: compare parent-level units and profit vs the parent's own normal day, against a similar whole-listing deal. Your split-test idea (enrol for part of the day) is the cleanest way to run it.
  • "Is S4's early spike one large order?" Yes, partly. One 31-unit order ($934) landed in the 03:00 PT hour. Without it, satin ran about 0.53× plan from 03:00 to 11:00 PT, which covers the deal going live at about 07:40 PT. Pace reads should strip bulk orders.
  • "How sure is the ad-spend verification?" The board's Marketing Stream matches the daily ad report to the cent. Sources still differ by $40–180 a day ($7,682 board, $7,821 Sellerboard, $7,859 ecotero) because of how shared campaigns are mapped to products, not because of a lag. Pick one source of record.
  • "Do the numbers include the deal fees?" Yes. The board's profit deducts $1,619 of fees ($100 per promotion + 1.5% of deal sales). The unplanned deals (US Satin Fitted, Cooling Pillowcases, Closet Organizer) cost about $500 in fees for about −$350 against a normal day.
