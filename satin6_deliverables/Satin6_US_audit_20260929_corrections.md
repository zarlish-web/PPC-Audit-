## Summary

The audit runs S6 as a mature product in the maintenance lane: cut prices, hold the top of search, test a few long-tail terms, and bring TACoS down toward a 12% ceiling. Cutting is the right direction for S6, but most of the audit's premises are wrong, and many of its rows either don't load or load something wrong. Review 45 endorsed the run after checking its arithmetic, not its premises.

**What we change, most important first:**

1. **The spend baseline is not S6's.** The engine reads $173.25 a day of spend and a 24.7% TACoS. S6's own spend over the same 14 days (15–28 Sep) was $88.43 a day in Command Center and $1,237.50 over the 14 days in Sellerboard ($88.39 a day). The engine's list includes Satin Fitted and Bamboo Sponsored Brands, and shared LTSF and brand product-target campaigns. It also leaves out the S4-named LTSF campaign that is S6's biggest spender. 2. **The margin is too high.** The engine prices every ceiling on $7.47 a unit (23.4% break-even). Sellerboard for the same window (31 Aug–30 Sep) gives $2.87 a unit all-in (9.0%) and $5.88 a unit excluding one-off charges (18.4%). Queen Black, the top seller, earns $1.91 all-in and $4.61 excluding one-offs. We price marginal clicks on the ex-one-off figure, and judge the product's profit on the all-in one.
3. **The advertised colours are wrong, and none of the colour swaps will load.** The engine's "hero" for Queen, Full and Twin is White, by an operator rule, not by sales. We hold every swap and every rename, and list the product-ad changes a person has to make by hand.
4. **King Red is the live money leak and has no re-point plan.** 35 campaigns advertise King Red, which has 10–13 units and runs out around 8–12 October. In Sellerboard it took $119.83 of ad spend on 15–28 Sep for 0 ad-attributed units. 32 of those campaigns stay on it after the load. ## 5. The lane, the focus and the goal

### 5.1 What the audit does

- The run is the **maintenance lane**: stage MATURE from the catalogue, press off, a 12% TACoS ceiling.
- Its **focus** is Satin, Satin Queen, Satin Full and Satin Twin. The focus is a job setting with no reason recorded.

  - 7 King terms declined as "out of focus".

### 5.2 What the data supports

**For not pushing:**
- Loss after ads in 12 of 13 months.
- Queen and Full convert at a half to a third of the market's rate like for like.
- S4 ranks better on most shared terms.
- The July deal's rank lift didn't last.

**The focus is on the weakest groups.** Satin Queen, Full and Twin are where S6 converts worst and where S4 is strongest. Silk King is the one group converting above the market (6.43% against 5.04%), and King is the only size profitable after ads. The audit tapers King because it is out of focus.

### 12.2 How confidence is measured: volume and placement first

A conversion or CTR read counts only when enough clicks sit behind it, and only if those clicks came from the placement being judged.

- **Top-of-search conversion.** The bar is 2.5 × the market's search-query conversion on the term; PPC's 7-day attribution runs 2–3 times the search-query figure. Our top-of-search conversion is trusted only when:
  - there are at least 50 top-of-search clicks, the bids document's own-rate point; and
  - there are enough clicks to separate our rate from the bar at 80% confidence.

  For example, "silk king sheets set" has a 10.9% top-of-search conversion on 18 clicks against a 14.1% bar. That needs 150 clicks: 132 more.
- **Search-query comparisons.** These count only when the market side has at least 30 purchases and 300 clicks, and our side has enough clicks to tell the difference. At a 5% market conversion rate that means 60–350 of our own clicks.
- **Placement.** A conversion read made mostly on product-page or rest-of-search clicks is not a top-of-search baseline. Where top of search is under 30% of a term's clicks, the fix is distribution, not price.
- **Terms below the thresholds are UNPROVEN.** They get no high or low score, just the clicks still needed and a dated test.
- **Where volume is adequate and performance is poor, the fix is Brand Management, not bids.** That means CTR or conversion below the market on enough clicks.

**Volume is what's missing on S6.** Across the 62 candidate terms, S6 bought 237 top-of-search clicks in September, 7.9 a day. No term has enough deal-free top-of-search clicks to trust its conversion rate; 11 pass only on the 90-day window, which contains the July and August deals.
