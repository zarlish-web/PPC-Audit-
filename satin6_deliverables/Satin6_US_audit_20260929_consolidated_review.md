## Summary

All three reviews reach the same verdict on the audit: its cuts, colour swaps and new campaigns should not load as written.
- **Both** reject the engine's White "hero" children and run ranking on the Black child of each size.
- **Both** price margin from Sellerboard, not the engine's 23.4%.
- **Both** build no new campaigns.
- **Both** see the drop as lost traffic, not worse conversion.
- **Both** hold deal days.

They differ on how hard to push, where, and on how bids reach the top of search. Where they agree, the shared call stands. Where they differ, this document takes the call with the better evidence and says why.

**What Review C adds:**
- a Satin King recovery case;

## 4. The consolidated plan

### 4.1 Goal and lane

**The goal isn't set.** Leadership's stated direction this week is profitability first. Joseph asked for the 30 Sep deal to be cancelled "due to profitability". Erik said margin is "greatly need[ed]", but also sees "tremendous untapped potential" for rank.

**Both reviews stay in the maintenance lane and leave the push size to the owners.** Review B sizes a 14-term push; Review A sizes Silk King tests. The consolidated default is:
- profit control on the terms S4 owns;
- holding what S6 already has in the top 20;

3. **Hero:** the size's best seller with 30 or more days of cover, not White by rule. Give King White its catalogue colour (A, B).
4. **Product ads:** read and write the product-ad roster so swaps load. Load back-up ads paused with a 14-day switch rule. Stop reading the advertised child from campaign names (A, B).
5. **Stock:** read Sellerboard stock and the shipment tracker in real time. Re-point any campaign whose only in-stock child falls under 14 days of cover (A, B).
6. **Deals:** read the deal calendar. Hold climbs, revives and launches on deal days. Exclude deal days from every read (A, B).

   - hold price on terms at target or inside the top 20 (A, B);
   - a sample gate of 15 clicks before any cut (B);
