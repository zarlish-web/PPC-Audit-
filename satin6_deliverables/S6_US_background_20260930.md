30 September 2026 · Command Center product 31 · parent ASIN B0D9QQWYP4 · 112 SKUs across Full, King, Queen and Twin in 29 colours · stage "Maintenance", no hero set · read-only background check, no preassumed goal

## Summary

What the data says, before any audit arrives:
1. **S6 loses money after ads in every window but the last week.** Sellerboard, 1–30 Sep: $20,127 of sales, profit before ads $1,815 (9.0%), after ads −$1,090 (−5.4%). Over 90 days: −$12,494 (−9.7%). It lost money in 12 of the last 13 months; only Feb 2026 was positive (+$269).
2. **Margin is much thinner than Command Center shows.** Command Center's list-price contribution is $9.59 a unit (28.9% break-even ACoS). Sellerboard's all-in figure for September is $2.88 a unit (9.0%), or $6.29 (19.7%) before one-off fees (LTSF, removal, inbound). On Queen Black, the top seller, it is $7.00 against $1.61.
3. **Ad spend was cut by about 70% at the end of August, and the engine logged none of it.** Spend: $10,111 over 2–31 Aug, $2,903 over 1–30 Sep. ACoS barely moved (31.3% → 29.9%). The engine has recorded no decisions on S6 in 60 or 180 days, and there is no S6 audit run on file.
4. **Sales fell with the ads.** Units per day: 44.8 over 90 days, 21.0 over 30, 17.5 over 14. Part of the earlier volume was deals and coupons ($5.9k of promotions in August), but the organic part fell too: non-ad sales went from about $710 a day in August to about $358 in September (Command Center).
5. **Organic rank has fallen on almost every head term.** 7-day medians, early July → last week: "satin sheets" 18 → 60, "silk sheets queen" 16 → 89, "satin sheets queen size" 19 → 45, "satin sheets king" 17 → 41. Only "king size silk sheets set" is at its target (5). The July Best Deal lifted ranks only while it ran.
6. **King is the one bright spot.** It is the only size profitable after ads in September (+$277, 4.0%). It is also the only size where we convert above the market like-for-like (Silk|King 6.43% against 5.04%, 90 days). Queen and Full convert at about half the market's rate.
7. **S6 is a small player, and S4 is its biggest rival for the same shoppers.** S6 has 0.90% of tracked niche traffic, against S4's 3.85%. 87% of S6's search terms are also S4's, S4 ranks better on 323 of the 415 both rank for, and each product's ads target the other's pages.
8. **Stock is heavy but in the wrong colours.** About 7,000 units ($81.7k at cost), over 330 days of cover. King Black alone has about 500 days. Meanwhile King Red, the #3 seller, has 13 units left (about 12 days), and 16 active SKUs are at 0.
9. **LTSF is material.** $6,884 was charged in the last 90 days. Slack estimates 792 aged units and about $854 for the 15 Oct charge.
10. **The structure has problems to clear before any push:**
    - Campaign names often don't match what they advertise.
    - An S4-named LTSF campaign is S6's biggest spender.
    - "satin sheets" exact has no live owner.
    - Exact campaigns send 37–42% of their clicks to product pages.
    - At the top of search S6 pays more per order than anywhere else (35–43% ACoS against about 22% in rest of search), the reverse of S4.

## 11. Questions to take into the audit

The data sets no goal on its own; these are the questions any plan for S6 has to answer:
1. **Who cut spend at the end of August, and why?** Was it a deliberate profitability call? With 12 of 13 months at a loss, a Sellerboard break-even of 9–20% and ACoS at 30%, cutting may be right. But organic sales fell with the ads.
2. **Which margin governs?** Sellerboard's is 9.0% all-in or 19.7% ex one-offs (September); Command Center's list-price figure is 28.9%. Any bid ceiling the audit sets on Command Center's figure will be too high.
