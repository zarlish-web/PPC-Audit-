The other 125 are replaced by the campaign settings below; most of them reach the same price by a different route. The rows that cite the team's B4 and B6 corrections apply those rules as written. Where the result is wrong, the fault is in the data under the rule, not the rule itself (appendix A).

The calls:
1. **Ranking: no price cuts on the ranking terms, and exact campaigns go top of search only** (boost 900%, base bid = the top-of-search bid ÷ 10, rest of search and product pages 0%, down-only bidding). The top-of-search bid is today's *effective bid* (base × (1 + boost)), not the cost per click. | Term | What it means here |
|---|---|

The top-of-search bid stays where it is; the other placements see only the small base bid. |
| Effective top-of-search bid | Base bid × (1 + top-of-search boost): the most we pay at the top. With down-only bidding the cost per click is at or below it, so it is the figure to hold. "Cost per click" means what was actually paid on average. |
| Market bound | The engine's own read: the campaign's top-of-search cost per click over 90 days + 15% (for example "king size silk sheets": $3.69 on 123 clicks, bound $4.24). | Like-for-like conversion | Amazon's search query data: our listing's purchases ÷ clicks against the whole market's, same weeks (30 Aug–26 Sep). Ad-reported conversion credits any colour bought within 7 days and runs 3–4 times higher, so it is compared only with other ad figures. |

## 3. The plan

### 3.1 One setting for every exact campaign: top of search only, on the bid, in two waves

Put the enabled exact campaigns on one setting: top-of-search boost 900%, base bid = the effective top-of-search bid ÷ 10, rest of search and product pages 0%, bidding "Dynamic – down only". How it is priced and when it goes out:
- **Price on the bid, not the cost per click.** The effective top-of-search bid (base × (1 + boost)) is what we offer; the cost per click is what we paid on average, and with down-only bidding it sits below the bid (for example "satin sheets queen" $3.01 against $3.87). A bid set at the average cost per click loses the auctions that cleared above it, about half of them.
  - On 20 ranking keywords a base set from the cost per click would have lowered the bid by 4–90%: "king size silk sheets" $4.60 → $3.40, "satin bed sheets full" $4.56 → $3.20, "king size satin sheet set" $3.90 → $3.00, "full satin sheets set" $4.40 → $3.50, "silk sheets full size" $2.56 → $2.20.
  - The workbook sets every base from the effective bid, and no ranking keyword is cut.
  - **Every live keyword is priced, not only the campaign's main keyword, in both waves.** Of the 290 campaigns going to 900%, 27 hold more than one live keyword (44 beside the first). Left at today's base, each would bid 10 times that base at the top: in the "queen size silk sheets" campaign, "queen satin sheets" at $1.99 would go to $19.90, and four zero-click keywords at $0.50 would each go to $5.00. The workbook gives every live keyword its own base (wave 1 in the bid column, wave 2 in the wave 2 setting column). Paused keywords get no write: "silk sheet" in the live "queen sheet silk" campaign and "king size sheets set silk" in the live "king size satin sheets with elastic" campaign stay paused (Section 5).

## Appendix A: fixes for the engine

The run applies the team's rules as written: 173 decisions cite the B4 corrections and 35 the B6 corrections, loaded into the engine on 28 Sep. Most of what went wrong comes from the data under the rules, not the rules. So the fixes below are to the engine's data and to tests the rules lack; the rules stay.

- **The impressions feed.** It reads 0 impressions on campaigns that spent (for example $279, $226 and $205 in 14 days, with 1,513 and 2,718 clicks in 90 days). The "not serving" rule then fires correctly on false data and freezes 103 campaigns (119 rows). Fix the feed; the rule is right.
- **The revive rule needs an ACoS test.** It revives on clicks and orders alone; every one of the 35 revives ran above break-even over 90 days. Add: revive only at or under the colour's break-even, or where the campaign owns a ranking term or clears an LTSF colour.
- **The size map.** It files 105 colours as "unsized" (Queen Black among them), makes Stone Grey the Queen hero and White King (0 units) the King hero. Its own OFF/ONTO line shows the result: off Queen Black (26,596 paid clicks, 14.3% conversion), onto Stone Grey (21 clicks, "thin, not read").
- **Size-changing switches.** Add the rule in Section 3.3: a size-named term never leaves its size; a colour-named term may, when its colour runs out.
- **The switch rows name the wrong current colour.** On rows 1924 and 1938 the switch row names a current colour (Queen Black; King Grey) that differs from the audit's own campaign record, which shows Full Black and Queen Black, as does Data Dive. The engine should build switch rows from the campaign record.
- **Deals.** It doesn't read the deal calendar (`engine_read` null) although the three deals are on it, and its margin window includes six days of the last deal.
- **Margin.** Use Sellerboard's realised margin per colour, at full price on normal days and at the deal price on deal days.
- **Focus scope.** It judges focus by syntax group, so the Silk group, "silk sheets" included, was tapered by rule rather than by its data.
- **Cuts on ranking terms.** The class steps cut ranking terms toward break-even in fixed percentages, although almost all advertising on this product runs above it. Price ranking terms on the effective top-of-search bid; where it sits above the market bound, hold it for a person, as the engine already does.
- **LTSF and back-ups.** It has no LTSF view, no back-up ranking colour, and no seasonality in its stock-out dates.
- **Paired rows.** Paired pauses turn terms off, and no-move boost rows break real cuts.
- **Forecast.** It prices test orders as spend ÷ historical ACoS: 105 orders a week on $47 a day is about 10% ACoS against the product's 36%, about four times overstated.
- **Conversion against the market.** Compare search-query conversion with search-query conversion, not ad-reported with search-query.
